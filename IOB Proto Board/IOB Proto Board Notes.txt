HP I/O Bus Prototyping Board Design Notes

This project was motivated by an ongoing effort to design and implement a modern(ish) equivalent to an HP 12908A Writable Control Store for an HP 2100A.  This required a base PCA designed to fit an HP 1000-series (211x, 2100, 21MX) I/O bus slot.  Lacking confidence in designing an "it just works" PCB for this project given that there would be necessary component (and corresponding design) revisions from the original it was decided to instead design a generic front-side wire-wrap Printed Circuit Assembly (PCA) around a uniform 0.1x0.1" matrix of plated thru-holes (PTH) without any predetermined power rail traces, thus affording a maximum of flexibility in size, position, and orientation of components -- particularly ICs.

Given the HP bus spacing of PCAs on 5/8" (15.88mm) centers this means that front-side wire-wrap pins must be comfortably shorter than this limit in order to account for PCB thickness (1.6mm) and probable solder-point extensions on the bottom of the adjacent PCB.  Examination of existing HP PCAs that include metal-cased transistors determined 9-10mm as the HP in-practice limit for component heights ... and thus front-sdie wire-wrap posts.  A higher limit could be accommodated by covering the bottom of the adjacent PCA with an insulating sheet (e.g., fish paper).  Or by keeping the next (upperside-adjacent) I/O slot empty and wiring the backplane as necessary to simulate the presence of a Jumper Card in that slot.  Alternatively, standard wire-wrap IC sockets could be employed at the cost of keeping the underside-adjacent I/O slot empty with the same proviso regarding backplane wiring modification.  Finally instead of using wire-wrap techniques one could use point-to-point wiring, likely on the front-side.

Standard SIL and DIL 0.1" (2.54mm) headers only provide 6mm of free post height, but after removing the 2-2.5mm plastic strip/block that holds the pins together to form the header then 8-8.5mm of free post height becomes available, meeting the MIL-STD-1130B "Connections, Electrical, Solderless Wrapped" two-level wrap specification.  To achieve the height objective, the easiest approach is to install (solder-in-place) the header strip/block upside-down just sufficiently deep (i.e., tip just showing on the bottom-side of the PCB) to achieve good mechanical stability in a plated-through PCB hole, and then carefully pry off the plastic strip/block from the remaining 3mm of post.  The result is an inexpensive set of wire-wrap posts that support a full two-level wire-wrap while remaining under the available inter-PCA plus safety clearance limit of 9-10mm.

Header strips/blocks are also available with longer pins capable of supporting three-level wrap, and even four-level wrap if the plastic strip/block is removed, but are significantly more expensive.  Instead, one can add some extra paired-posts to serve as 1-in to 3-out intermediate connections to augment the limited 1-in to 1-out daisy chain connections supported by two-level posts should it become desirable to introduce branch points in any daisy-chained signals.  Adjacent thru-hole pads can be joined with solder plus a small wire on the back-side of the PCA; this method also supports connections between IC/component positions and adjacent front-side wire-wrap posts.

MIL-STD-1130B, Figure 3 "Diagonal Dimension", specifies the smallest square post dimension as nominally 0.025" (0.635mm) with a diagonal measurement ranging between 0.0325" (0.8255mm) and 0.0355" (0.9017mm).  Standard SIL and DIL 0.1" (2.54mm) headers have 0.65mm square posts (thus 0.92mm diagonally).  These dimensions are very close to those specified in the MIL-STD and thus can be used with standard wire-wrap tools.  If available, slightly more expensive gold-plated SIL-header posts will provide better wire-wrap contact and resistance to oxidation than the common tin-plated types.  

The standard PCB plated-through hole interior diamater is nominally 1mm, resulting in a fairly loose fit intended to ease alignment and insertion of long SIL/DIL strips and rigid-body connectors.  Since our SIL header-based wire-wrap posts will eventually have their alignment/support plastic strip removed after soldering in place it is desirable to have a mechanical fit that is as tight as possible before soldering, even at the cost of some "fiddling" and non-zero insertion force during assembly.  A hole diameter of 0.8mm (centered in a 1.6mm diameter pad) provides a reasonably snug fit and was specified for all locations intended for use by wire-wrap posts.  (Note that this assertion is based on experience with PCB prodution at JLCPCB; it's possible that the production methods at other manufacturers will result in different outcomes as regards "fitness".)

PCB dimensions, including precise positioning of top-edge and bottom-edge connectors, PCB ejector holes, and trace offsets from side edges, were verified against a diverse set of on-hand PCAs from multiple HP design and  manufacturing eras.  The final design measures 196mm by 220.8mm.  The PCA top-edge includes the standard HP 48-pin I/O connector; the bottom-edge the standard HP 86-pin I/O Bus connector.

The interior matrix of PTH is square, being 72 positions wide by 72 positions tall.  Additional uncommitted PTH positions appear in two rows adjacent to the top and bottom edges of the interior matrix, interspersed with PTH positions connected by traces to pins on edge connectors.

Positions 3 thru 84 the 86-pin bottom-edge connector are labeled on the front silkscreen in accordance with the HP 1000-series I/O bus specification.  Note that some details of the I/O Bus specification and signal acronyms evolved over time (these are documented in a related project, including variations in the supplied Vcc in different CPU models); maximally clear acronyms were chosen for use on the silkscreen.  Clusters of signals are outlined on the front silkscreen in order to clearly distinguish their associated wire-wrap post positions from adjacent uncommitted PTH.

Each bottom-edge connector position connects to one, or in the case of the +4.85v and -2v power rails multiple, nearby PTH position.  The front +4.85v rail (pin 39) connects to eight PTH while the rear +4.85v rail (pin 40) connects to seven PTH for a total of 15 wire-wrap posts for use in +4.85v power distribution.  The front -2v rail (pin 47) connects to three PTH while the rear -2v rail (pin 48) connects to four PTH for a total of seven wire-wrap posts for use in -2v power distribution.  Including the two wire-wrap posts for +12v power distribution and two wire-wrap posts for -12v power distribution there are a total of 26 wire-wrap posts available for power distribution.  There are an additional 36 wire-wrap posts located along the lateral edges of the PCB for use as GND connections or endpoints for electrolytic/tantalum power smoothing capacitors.  Finally there are a total of eight wire-wrap post locations near the top-edge connector for use as GND connections shared with external devices.

Consideration was given to use of the prototyping board in non-IOB slots which may have different signal and power assignments to the 86-pin bottom-edge connector.  Although it is normally the case that opposed pairs of power and ground rails are bonded together at one or more locations on a PCA, the prototype board was designed to avoid this bonding so that all 86-pins are electrically isolated from each other.  Pins 1, 2, 85, and 86, usually assigned as GND connections, are independentally routed around the periphery of the PCB while including eleven non-PTH locations that can be used to bond pin 1 and 2 traces together using short soldered-in-place wires; the same pattern applies to pin 85 and 86 traces.  The four standard power rails (+12v, +4.85v, -2v, and -12v) may be bonded together front-to-back using a short wire between applicable adjacent post locations, if desired.  In all six of these power/ground rails it is usually the case that the backplane incorporates sufficient bonding between pairs of opposing power rail pins so that additional bonding on the PCB is not strictly necessary.

The PCB top-edge includes the standard HP I/O 48-pin edge-connector.  These pins connect to a set of five 2x5 DIL header locations (with standard-dimension PTH) that may be used to host straight or right-angle IDC connectors in the case that an alternative means to access (possibly a fewer number of) signals than the standard HP connector may host is desirable (or simply that ribbon-type IDC cables are desirable).  These pins further connect to a directly corresponding set of five 2x5 wire-wrap post PTH.  The IDC 2x5 blocks are identified on the front silkscreen with the IDC positions including orientation marks and identification of the corresponding edge-connector positions (1 thru 24, and A thru BB).  The corresponding set of five 2x5 wire-wrap post PTH are also outlined on the front silkscreen.

When producing this design the base material is FR-4, thickness 1.6mm, outer copper weight 1 oz, the surface finish is ENIG plus 1U" gold edge-connector plating.  30 degree bevels on the edge connectors is recommended.
